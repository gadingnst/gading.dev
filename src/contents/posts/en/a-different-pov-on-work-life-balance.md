*Work-Life Balance* is about balancing your work life and your personal life. Right, that's basically what the term means. You can tell from the words alone: *Balance*.

In this short piece I'm just sharing a bit of my own point of view on *Work-Life Balance*. If you agree, take what's useful. If you don't, no problem. Everyone has their own point of view. Okay? 🙆🏻

Ah, *Work-Life Balance*... a term that's a hot topic these days. Yes, because some people get to experience it. But some haven't, because they have to work out there whether they like it or not. And some aren't lucky enough to get a job at all, and some have even lost theirs.

---

In practice, *Work-Life Balance* is always taken to mean, or tied to, separating work life from personal life so they stay balanced and don't interfere with each other. But is that what it takes to reach balance? Not really.

Does separating work life and personal life so they don't interfere with each other guarantee that we live balanced and happy? I'd say not necessarily.

*It's pointless if we've done a great job separating work life from personal life so they're balanced and don't interfere with each other, but when we're actually living that work life, we feel forced and unhappy. In other words, we're only happy in our personal life, and stressed or unhappy in our work life. Can you call that balanced? How is the happiness not balanced?*

*After all, the whole point of reaching Work-Life Balance is to be happy, right?*

If that happens to us, we end up as people who look for happiness through constant *self-reward* in our personal life. To put it bluntly: *work until you're stressed, then use the money from work to get rid of the stress.* And we keep going around that loop every month. *Ironic*, isn't it?

Yes, stress can't be avoided and it can happen to anyone. But at least we can reduce how much of it we get, so we're not stressed all the time and can be happy in our work life too.

---

From this point of view, *Work-Life Balance* can actually come from our own mindset and how we treat ourselves: can we be *curious* about our work, build a good work environment, choose and spend time with supportive coworkers, and manage our time between work and our personal and family life. Remember, ***managing*** doesn't mean ***separating***.

I once talked with a friend of mine, the CTO at the office, about how to spend the weekend. I asked him how he spends his weekends, and he said: working on a side hustle. Why? He said his own version of *Work-Life balance* is working! Yes, he kind of turned work into his hobby, his personal pleasure. I was stunned.

When I thought about it, he had a point. If we love our work, or our hobby is our work, it won't feel like work. I've always liked this quote:

> *"Do a work what you love, and you will never work a day in your life."* - Marc Antony

---

So back to our topic, ***a POV on Work-Life Balance***. *Work-Life balance* can actually come from our own mindset and how we treat ourselves, and that's different for every person. We have to find the key to our own version of *Work-Life balance*.

In my view there's one thing people often forget when trying to reach *Work-Life balance*: finding their [Ikigai](https://www.google.com/search?q=apa+itu+ikigai), the point where we meet 4 elements that can make our life more balanced, happier, and even meaningful to the people around us. I won't go deep into *Ikigai* here, but I'll summarize the 4 elements briefly:

###### 1. Passion (*What you love*).
*Passion* means something we enjoy. Something that makes us eager to do it and happy when we do it, like a hobby or a personal pleasure.

###### 2. Mission (*What world need*).
*Mission* is what the world around us needs. It may not match our passion, but that doesn't rule out contributing to our surroundings with the small things we can do.

###### 3. Vocation (*What you can get paid for*).
There's no denying that to survive, we need an income. *Vocation* is something we can do that earns us money.

###### 4. Profession (*What you are good at*).
*Profession* is something we feel we're an expert at. We can get that expertise through education or training courses.

![Ikigai Diagram](/media/blog/pov-tentang-work-life-balance-yang-berbeda/ikigai.jpeg)

Yep, ***Ikigai***. To me, *Ikigai* is one of the important doors that lets the light of *Work-Life Balance* in. By opening this *Ikigai*, we understand better what *Work-Life balance* is, and our reason for living each day. Do you feel like you've found the *Ikigai* in your life? Or are you still looking for the key to open that door?

So, where people usually say *Work-Life Balance* means separating work life and personal life so they don't "interfere" with each other, the different point of view in this piece is how we make ourselves "love" our work life, so it doesn't become something that "interferes" with our personal life. That's why I brought *Ikigai* into this piece.

> By doing what we love, and getting paid for it, we've already reached *Work-Life balance* itself. If we can't do that, try to find something we can love and get paid for. If we can't do that either, try to love the work we have now.
