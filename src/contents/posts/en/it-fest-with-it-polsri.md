At the time, we were in a Multimedia class and the lecturer was still teaching. Suddenly a group of students from the MI (Informatics Management) department came in to let us know that their department's annual festival was coming up soon. Yes, the festival was called `IT FEST`. Long story short, they explained the events, and then one of them caught our interest: the `Web Programming` competition.

The 4 of us wanted to enter it. Unfortunately, the competition was limited to a maximum of 2 people per team. After talking it over for a while, we decided to split into 2 teams. I teamed up with Irvan, and Arief teamed up with Aji.

The competition also didn't allow participants to use a Framework or a **CMS**. That's where we got confused: what kind of Framework wasn't allowed, and was a Library like **jQuery** still okay? A lot of questions came up in our heads, so we decided to ask the organizing committee, but their answers didn't fully answer our questions. In the end, we decided to ask at the `Technical Meeting` to get more detailed answers.

### Technical Meeting
A few days later, on a Sunday, the `Technical Meeting` was held at `Gramedia Pusat Palembang`. That's where we saw the faces of the programmers we'd be competing against. They came from different departments, and some were even from another university, **UNSRI**. During the TM, there was one student who seemed really skilled at `Web Programming`. He asked 'Can we use **Socket.io**? **Redis**?' and a lot more. It looked like his Team was going to use advanced tech in their app. Unfortunately we didn't know where that student with all the questions was from.

After a while, the committee's final decision at the TM was to build a web app themed `E-Learning` in less than 2 weeks, with **BACKEND FRAMEWORKS** like **Laravel**, **Codeigniter** and the like not allowed. We felt a little relieved there, because we could still use a **Frontend Framework** for styling the UI. Alhamdulillah, with such a short time, less than two weeks, there's no way we'd design the UI from scratch with native **CSS** lol.

### Coding~
The next day we went to the Library to put together which **stacks** we'd use for our app. At that point we didn't really have a picture yet of what kind of `E-Learning` app to build. But right then ideas started coming, and in the end Irvan and I decided to build an `Online Library` app, while Arief and Aji built an `Online School` app. Long story short, we finished putting the **stack** together and finally started coding.

We went through a lot of challenges and experiments while building this `E-Learning` app. From using the **MVC** architecture concept to designing tables full of relations and more. Oh yeah, in this App we used a framework we built ourselves, **Felis Database**. Yeah, a tiny Framework based on **OOP and PDO**, good enough to handle basic **CRUD** on the database hehe.

We also didn't forget to build website security for this app, because it was part of the scoring too. For security we used a **CSRF Token**, email confirmation, password encryption and input validation.

Days went by, and we worked on the project through the ups and downs. Every day we often slept late, plus we still had our duties as students, doing assignments. And on D-1 before the `Presentation`, the competition committee told us to **deploy** the app and submit the presentation file and the app's source. <del>That day we poured out all our energy and chakra because we were chasing the deadline</del>. We didn't sleep all night because of that deadline.

### Presentation
Then the moment came: the last step of the `Web Programming IT FEST` championship was the `Presentation`. We had to take responsibility for the app we'd built. We had to be able to explain everything if the judges or other participants asked.

Arief and Aji went up first, presenting the features of their website. What brought us down was when the judges asked Arief and Aji what made this website better than other `E-Learning` sites.

Long story short, our turn came. Irvan and I finally went up front. Irvan calmly opened the presentation, while I was still nervous about speaking. My job was just to explain the technical side and demo the app. During the demo, we showed all the features in our app, down to the input validation, to make it look wow haha. Because our app let users upload any book, the judges immediately criticized the **Copyright** issue. Fair enough: users could upload licensed books, and that would end up as a copyright problem. After a bit of defending ourselves, we finally said 'Yes, maybe that's beyond our scope, sir'. In the end the judge praised our idea because it was pretty creative, but because of that issue, the judge suggested we develop the app further going forward.

In the end, the ones I thought would be our toughest competition in the judging were the team from Unsri, the 3rd-semester juniors, and the team from the MI department itself. Their app features were good and plentiful, and the way they presented deserved a thumbs up. But it seemed the participant who asked about **Socket.io** didn't show up for the presentation.

### Beyond Expectations
And then the day came. The `IT FEST` seminar was held on Monday around 8, but as competitors we only had to come in the afternoon after the break. So we arrived at `Graha POLSRI` at half past 1 in the afternoon. Long story short, the committee started announcing the competition winners. <del>That was also when our hearts started pounding, is this a sign of love?</del> Oops, Skip. Hahaha.

Honestly, we were confident we'd get at least 2nd or 3rd place. Because we were sure first place would go to the MI team, since their design, app features and presentation looked more perfect than ours.

Then the 3rd place winner was announced: the team from Unsri. No doubt they placed, since they'd performed well in the presentation.

Then the 2nd place winner was announced: the team of juniors from our own department. That's where our hope of winning started to fade; Irvan and I already assumed we wouldn't place, because we were sure the MI team would be the winner.

And first place goes to.. **'Team IT-B !!!'**

Huh, hearing that, Irvan and I looked at each other. Confusion popped up, plus nerves when we were told to go up on stage as representatives of the winning team. I still couldn't believe we were the winners.

After the prizes and the trophy were handed out, we took photos and shook hands with the event committee.

![Me on stage. Btw, I'm the one with glasses, hehe.](/media/blog/it-fest-bersama-it-polsri/4.jpeg)

Finally we came down from the stage. Pride and shock rolled into one as we came down. After that, my team and I took photos to document this win. A truly unforgettable proud moment for `IT Polsri`, <del>so dramatic</del>.
