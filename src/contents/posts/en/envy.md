### Overview

Before getting into my take on ENVY, I want to tell a little story and joke around a bit first. So here's the thing: I'm sure everyone here uses Instagram, and maybe some of you use Twitter too. Yeah, I'd dare say both of those social networks are places to *show off*.

From the perspective I see, there are some classic *behaviors* and *cultures* around **Instagram** VS **Twitter**. Instagram is where people show off ***wealth***, ***jobs***, ***travelling***, and other wow stuff. But Twitter is the opposite: people show off ***being broke***, ***being unemployed***, ***being lazy***, and other silly stuff. Total opposites.

So the users of these two social networks ended up with different characters:

*Instagram users are more serious, touchy and cocky. Twitter users are sillier, bored and all over the place*. Hahaha, this section is just for fun, okay.

That's what first made me pay attention to how social media users *behave*, and it varies a lot. That was when I thought: there's no good *behavior* we get from using social media, so how did scrolling it become a habit? Every day there's always some time spent opening social media. Well, let's get started..

---

### Background

I used to be the type who got envious easily, but luckily I was aware of it. That's why I've **always** (still to this day) never followed celebrity accounts on Instagram, for the sake of my peace of mind. Instead of worrying about how to be like them, it's better to work hard to become the person we want to be.

I know Instagram isn't where I make money, even though a lot of people do make money there. I use Instagram just for fun. It's not really where I build my portfolio either. But back then I thought: if I can spend so much time on Instagram and feel envious when I see people show off what they have, why can't I do the same? But what would I even show off.

But there's one thing I want to tell you. Actually, one of my motivations for building this website and starting to write a blog was that very ENVY.

*Whoa, A's website is cool, and their portfolio is huge..*

*Damn, B is awesome, sharing what they know through a blog..*

*Wow, they're good at writing docs, and their writing is great too..*

*Damn, their github stats are all green, what are they contributing to..*

*So envious of C, this person keeps building projects..*

That's where my wish to be like them came from, but I had to be able to set limits on myself, figuring out what I could and couldn't do. Because if I want to be like them but can't set limits on myself, I'm the one who suffers; ***It's hard to be someone else***.

At first I was always envious of, and put off by, the same old *feeds* on social media. But this time there was a different turning point: there were platforms whose *feeds* actually motivated me to be like those people, and changed my view of the word **show off**. Which social networks? [**GitHub**](https://github.com) and [**Linkedin**](https://linkedin.com).

Yep, these two are pretty different. I could call them places to show off too. But what people show off there are ***contributions***, ***work***, ***projects*** and ***productivity***. And the ***envy*** they give you feels different too.

When I look at the *feeds* on github or linkedin: *"wow these people are seriously cool"*. I want to be in their ranks. And there, at the same time, I can build my personal portfolio too.

When I want to find inspiration, there are the ***Explore*** and ***Trending*** tabs. My first impression looks like:

> *I should focus on, this! Here's my passion. Stop worrying about what others share, instead "talk is cheap, show me your code!" - Linus Torvalds*.

I got ***envious*** of people who contribute a lot, especially the ones who show up in my *feed/timeline*. I want to be in their ranks too, and if I'm lucky, make other people ***envious*** too, so they're moved to follow this kind of envy down this path, hahaha.

But there are some things I always wonder about when I see the contributions and projects they make.

*Since when have they been learning, how are they so productive..*

*How long did it take them to build that..*

*How do they learn, to get that much knowledge..*

*Do they spend money to learn too, how much..*

*Is it comparable to me..*

---

### Takeaway

So since then I took the initiative to build and write a blog on this website. Not just here: on Instagram too, I tried to shape my surroundings and interactions on my account by *following* people who are active and productive in the coding world, so I get new *insight* and inspiration from the work they share. And of course I also show off my own work and activities. I also share knowledge and a few jokes about the coding world with my *followers*, both to satisfy myself and to give some new *insight* to the people who see it.

And sure enough, my Instagram account is now fairly active, with interactions with people who want to share something or ask something. I'm happy to help them with answers I know, and I also get insight from people with more experience when I share a problem. So basically we share with each other. Why does it work like that? Because I built a different *behavior* on my Instagram account, around a field I actually like.

But since then I realized that the ones in the wrong aren't the people showing off, it's me being too ***insecure*** about their *feeds* on social media. And I started changing my own view of the word ***show off***. Now I see social media as a place where people *express* & *appreciate* what they've achieved in their lives, although some of it is also just absurd fun, lol.

Yes, it means they're proud of themselves after achieving it, so they show it off. That's what social media was made for anyway, to put it nicely, *self-expression*. You know the status form on ***facebook*** or ***twitter***, the placeholder literally says: *what's on your mind?*. I also once saw a reel by [Rico Huang](https://www.instagram.com/rcohuang/) about social media [here](https://www.instagram.com/p/CeYrvgHNK23/), check it out yourself if you have time.

And really, our job as the ones watching is to *appreciate*, or at least *stay quiet*, not to *trash-talk*. Yes, I know ***envy*** is always there, ***But c'mon, let them live***. At least our *behavior* toward them changes, from *trash-talker* to *appreciator*.

*Rather than letting this envy pollute how we think about them, let's turn it into appreciation for them, even if it's just in our heads. Or better yet, turn this envy into motivation! **Learn to appreciate people, and people will appreciate us too**.*

And in the end, my view of ***showing off*** is now quite different from before. *Because people changes* too, right, haha. Now when I see someone expressing themselves, about their wealth, their success, and so on, what's always on my mind is: *"I don't know how much time they spent, how much money they put in, and how much they struggled and sacrificed to get there."*

> *We don't really know about others background. So, let them live! As long as they don't break others down, we must apreciate that, or at least we don't disturb them*

And now I have a target to contribute every weekend, whether for myself or for the community. But sometimes it stalls because I don't have time or I'm just not in the mood, lol. So I get that *Work-life Balance* feeling, and since I haven't found a hobby other than being in front of a screen, I'm enjoying this one for now. Okay, let's get started. What should I build? What should I fix? What should I contribute? The answer is in ourselves. Can't find inspiration? Come find me, lol.

Well, go look for *insight*, on the social networks I mentioned earlier for example.
