Humans are perfect beings, created by Allah with Reason. Reason is one of the gifts from our God that makes us perfect beings. As long as we use it well, of course.

One part of reason is ***Thinking***. Who doesn't know what thinking is? Thinking is something we need as normal humans to find answers to the problems in our lives.

By definition, Thinking is brain activity that connects the bits of knowledge and information in our brain so we can form a decision or an answer. To explain, look at the following exercise.

$1 + 1 = ?$

$7 - 2 = ?$

$2 * 5 = ?$

We can obviously find the answers to these easily. That's roughly what a simple example of ***experience*** looks like: the answers are already stored in our brain. So we just pull the answer out without having to think any longer, because we've seen these problems many times. It's different if you give them to a kid who just started school. They'll need more time to find the answers, because they don't have experience with these problems yet.

Then what about these?

$429 \times (7!) = ?$

$69321 + \sqrt{25} = ?$

$12732 \div \frac{2^{\sqrt{2^3}}}{2} = ?$


Yep, these are harder and take more time to answer. That's because we've probably never seen these problems before. But here's the interesting part: even though we haven't met these problems before, we can still solve them using the small basics of knowledge and experience we already have. This process is what we call thinking.

From these two simple examples, we can see that this is how we go about finding answers to the problems in our lives. When we face a problem we've met before, we reflexively use the answers already stored in our brain. And when we face a problem we've never met, we reflexively use the small basics of knowledge and experience we have.

But what if we don't have any of those basics at all? Clearly, in that situation we need a ***Mentor***. Picture the kid who just started school facing the first set of problems: they clearly need a mentor to guide them through it.

---

Okay, now to the main topic. This piece isn't about how to think fast or anything like that. Here we'll talk about how to improve the quality of our thinking.

There's an interesting way to improve the quality of our thinking. ***[Dr. Edward De Bono](https://www.google.com/search?q=Edward+the+bono)*** describes a thinking method in his book ***["Six Thinking Hats"](https://www.google.com/search?q=six+thinking+hats)***: there are 6 ways humans think. In the book, these 6 ways of thinking are pictured as 6 colored Hats:

- White Hat,
- Red Hat,
- Yellow Hat,
- Black Hat,
- Green Hat, and
- Blue Hat

Each Hat represents a point of view or a way of thinking. Here are the points of view for each thinking Hat:

![Six Thinking Hats](/media/blog/meningkatkan-kemampuan-berfikir/six-thinking-hats.jpg)

### 1. White Hat
The White Hat represents thinking that is neutral, factual and objective. When we wear this Hat, we're collecting data or information that is factual and objective, and that information becomes the foundation for solving a problem. Information here doesn't include opinions or personal interpretations, like: *'I think..', 'They say..', 'As far as I know..',* and so on.

Examples of statements we can use while wearing the White Hat:
- Is this data valid?
- Where does it come from?
- Has this method been proven?
- How many people have tried it?
- Based on the statistics we have...
- As the facts show...

Overall, the White Hat is the most basic Hat to have. It can be the foundation for the other Hats, because the quality of every decision or answer we make from here on depends on the quality of the data and information we have.

### 2. Red Hat
The Red Hat represents thinking that is emotional: instinct, intuition and feelings. When we wear this Hat, we approach a problem from the point of view of emotion, instinct and feelings, positive or negative. Using instinct, we often get a sense of direction about things that can't be expressed as facts and information.

Not everything in this world has data. That's why this Hat matters too. Emotion, instinct and feelings play an important role in judging and expressing things that have no data. Obviously, because we're human. Without the Red Hat's point of view, we'd be like robots that only make decisions from data and set aside human feelings. Should we just not make a decision when we face a problem with no data? Of course not, right?

Examples of statements we can use while wearing the Red Hat:
- My instinct says no to this decision.
- I think this decision is too heavy to carry out without exploring it more deeply.
- People might not agree with this decision.
- This decision is too cruel to carry out.

Still, we can't make decisions and answer problems with emotion alone. We also have to combine feelings with the facts or data we have, and gather intuitions from different people. Otherwise the decision we make could be wrong and the consequences could be very serious.

### 3. Yellow Hat
The Yellow Hat represents thinking that is positive, constructive and optimistic, with hopes of success, strength, benefit or gain. When we wear this Hat, we think about the benefits of the decisions we've made, and ask why this decision or answer is worth exploring and implementing.

Examples of statements we can use while wearing the Yellow Hat:
- What are the positives?
- What are the benefits?
- Why is this decision worth making?
- How can this decision help us going forward?

The Yellow Hat's point of view helps us keep going when everything looks hard. We can clearly see the positive sides of the decision we made. As a result, we're more motivated to solve the problem.

### 4. Black Hat
The opposite of the Yellow Hat, the Black Hat represents thinking that is negative, destructive and pessimistic, with expectations of failure, weakness or loss. When we wear this Hat, we have to think critically to examine the risks that could come from the decision or answer we've made.

Examples of statements we can use while wearing the Black Hat:
- What are the negatives?
- What are the losses?
- Why might this decision not work?
- How could this decision bring us down going forward?

The Black Hat's point of view actually helps us be more careful when facing a problem. Not careless and reckless. It's easier to take steps toward solving a problem once we know the weak spots and what could make us fall when we pick a decision.

### 5. Green Hat
The Green Hat represents thinking that is creative, alternative or *out of the box*. When we wear this Hat, we let our brain collect as many creative ideas as possible without weighing the information, facts, logic and negative possibilities of those ideas. The goal is to keep our brain from getting blocked.

The Green Hat's point of view helps us grow and act on creative ideas to create innovation and new perspectives. When using the Green Hat, we first have to eliminate the negative possibilities the Black Hat detected. This Hat can also produce decisions or alternative ways to handle the problems we identified with the Black Hat.

### 6. Blue Hat
The Blue Hat represents thinking about planning, process, organizing and action. The 5 Hats above help us *break down* the components of the decision we're going to make. With the Blue Hat, we think about how to implement those components.

This Hat leans toward management and action. Using the Blue Hat helps us execute decisions. All the decision components we've built with the 5 Hats above are wasted if there's no management and action behind them.

---

The 6 thinking Hats above can improve the quality of our thinking and help us solve the problems we're facing. None of the 6 Hats is more important than the others. They all matter equally.

Normally, not everyone thinks this way, and not everyone can use all 6 points of view. It's like owning 6 hats but preferring to wear one or a few colors every day, without ever wearing the others.

But we can still use all these Hats if we keep practicing. A good way to think is to use all the Hats *step by step*. The decisions or answers we make will be more mature if we use all these ways of thinking.

Even if we can't use all of them right now, we can practice until using all of them becomes a habit. So starting now, let's get used to using these 6 ways of thinking before solving the problems in our daily lives ☺️
