From the moment we first sit in a classroom, whether it's kindergarten or elementary school, we're always pushed to chase achievements. We always want (or are pushed?) to be ranked number 1, we want to win this competition, that competition, that activity, and this activity. Yeah, when we're little our goal is probably not much more than making our parents happy, then asking them to buy us something...

But as we grow into teenagers, our reasons for chasing achievements get wider. It's not just about making our parents happy. On top of that, sometimes the goal is just to be recognized by the people around us as smart and capable, or to put it bluntly, to get attention. And the funny part: to catch the attention of our school crush.

As we grow into adults, our reasons get even wider. What started as getting attention now branches out again: getting into university, getting a scholarship, and getting a job. In fact, as adults we realize that nobody was really paying attention to us from childhood through our teens, except our parents.

So, if I had to sum it up:
- Kid -> Make your parents happy, then ask them to buy you something.
- Teenager -> Get attention from the people around you (classmates, teachers, your crush and so on) so you're recognized, well known and popular.
- Adult -> At this stage we care more about ourselves, and don't care about the people around us as long as we're successful.

---

All of my conclusions above are my own view and not always valid, but I'm sure it's like that for most people. This piece is actually something that's been on my mind for a long time. ***So, are achievements useful?***

Here we could probably answer: ***Yes, for ourselves***. Looking at my 3 points above, we can say they're useful for ourselves. But not for other people.

Then what about the achievements of people who have contributed to society? Isn't that useful for other people too?

Okay, let's say there's someone who has served society by making something people can use and benefit from, and then they get an achievement for it. What benefits other people, ***the achievement*** or ***the contribution***?

I'll say it loud and clear: "Obviously the ***Contribution***".

Well, there's no arguing with it, that's just how it is. Our achievements don't actually benefit other people. Our achievements have no impact on other people. We join this and that activity to chase certificates, we join this and that competition to chase awards, and worse, some people are willing to do stupid things to get public attention.

---

Everything I mentioned above is nothing more than *Achievement*. Yes, everyone has the right to be happy about what they've achieved, and an achievement is no more than a reward for yourself. ***So there's nothing wrong with chasing achievements. But make sure your contribution also benefits other people, okay!***
