## Background

At first I was just a vocational high school (SMK) kid with a TKJ (computer and network engineering) major label, who honestly had zero knowledge about computers or technology, only a hobby of playing games at the time. Besides, <del>the SMK was a private one too.</del> Honestly, I only went to that SMK because I'd taken the public high school entrance test twice and failed both times. Lololol.

But I'm really grateful for the path Allah gave me back then. I learned a lot there, I got a lot of knowledge there. Not all of my learning came from school, maybe just the basics. But those basics are what made me curious and kept me digging deeper into technology, right up to the moment I'm writing this article.

Back at the end of 2013, if I remember right, my dark side was reinstalling operating systems, especially when Windows 8 was new and hot, and everyone was hunting for cracked copies. Maybe because it was fun surfing Google until you found what you were looking for. And once you got it? The feeling, man. It was prestigious to use Windows 8, to show it off to my classmates even though my laptop was a potato, but the fun part was that I could make them jealous enough to want it too, and in the end I got a **side job**, lololol. Not just my classmates, people from other classes sometimes came to me to troubleshoot their laptops too. Why did that happen? Because of that curiosity! Btw, 6 years ago finding cracks was hard, not like now when they're everywhere.. just type it and it probably shows up as the first result.

---

I wasn't the type who liked dating in school, who got moody over love problems, let alone cried because someone hurt me. I was just someone absorbed in my own world of games and my reinstall jobs. Sure, I looked like a nerd because of it, but because of it I could also earn my own pocket money, even if it wasn't much; not bad for a teenager my age at the time. I did get moody because of it, and I ran into plenty of problems because of it too, not love problems, but <del>**problems with people's laptops crashing while I was installing drivers and software on them.**</del> Oops, busted, lololol. But they got fixed in the end, hehe.

In 3 years at that school I learned so many things: reinstalling, hardware, networking, troubleshooting, crimping tools.. Hahaha. Until I finally met coding in 11th grade. And even that gave me a headache. I had no idea what I was typing, no idea what each line of code I typed meant, no idea what those weird symbols I typed were for. All I knew was copying what my teacher typed, *"its works ? i don't know why, its fail ? i don't fucking know why!"* And at that time I kind of hated that subject. I only liked learning troubleshooting and reinstalling, and one more, building PCs. Maybe because that's where I made money back then, and because my hobby was gaming, I was always looking for ways to boost the performance of my laptop and computer even though they started out with potato-chip specs, Hahaha.

Btw, the first programming language I learned in SMK, the one that gave me a headache, was PHP. HTML isn't a programming language, so I still enjoyed it even though it gave me a headache too, Hahaha. Back then we practiced in notepad, bro, lol, and after a few months the teacher told us to move to Dreamweaver. **Facing errors? Often. Not understanding? Always.** Hahaha.

## My way start from here!

After graduating from SMK with a TKJ major, I got into POLSRI with my first-choice program, Computer Engineering. Why did I choose that program? Nothing more than my background! I assumed I'd learn more deeply about the stuff I learned in SMK: troubleshooting, networking, hardware, building PCs and so on. But.. What the fuck? I ran into electronics theory that kept leading back to physics and calculations, and those were subjects I hated even more than the programming lessons back in SMK.

And worse, on campus I ran into programming courses, one of the subjects I hated back in SMK. **Ever felt like you picked the wrong major? Once!** But well, I just went along with it; the program was my own choice anyway. Why regret it? Isn't it our own fault for choosing it? So should we blame Fate? Like Kang Pukis says, "Just go along with it.."

So I kept taking the courses on campus while still gaming, tinkering with PCs, and doing my reinstall jobs whenever someone came by. But.. hey, as time went on and I kept taking the programming courses on campus, I started rethinking whether to keep hating them. Like the saying "practice makes perfect", which I'll tweak a bit to "love grows from meeting often", lol, hahaha, okay skip.

Then, little by little I came to like the programming courses, because of the way the lecturers on campus taught, Algorithms included. Different from my programming teacher in SMK, who just showed us finished code and told us to type it without explaining the flow. Sure, the lecturers on campus also only taught the basics. What matters is I got knowledge there. Because we kept getting problem-solving exercises, I got more diligent about experimenting in the coding world.

---

Time passed, years passed.. I liked programming more and more. My curiosity is what took me to dive deeper into the ocean of programming. I finally found friends in class who shared the same struggle and the same hobby. We learned and fought together, until we finally thought of making our own TEAM. With that team we became more committed and more confident to learn even deeper. We joined competitions and events, and built apps together to grow our team's good name.
